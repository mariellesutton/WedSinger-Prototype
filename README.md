WedSinger — Wedding Singer Booking Prototype
1. Problem, Persona, Capability, and Value
Need:
 Singer/pianist duos have a ready-to-perform act but may struggle to find weddings when they do not yet have a wedding network.
Persona:
 A singer/pianist duo that has everything ready to perform but needs a way to find weddings and become known to couples looking for live music.
Primary Capability:
 WedSinger matches wedding singers with couples looking for live music. Singers can view matches, see wedding details, communicate with couples, and book through the app.
Fundamental Value:
 Visibility. WedSinger helps ready-to-book singers become discoverable to couples actively looking for wedding music.

3. Design Question Plan
Need
 Question: “Think about the last time you were trying to find a wedding to sing at. What did you do to try to find one?”
 Prediction: They will likely rely on personal connections, social media, or word of mouth because they do not yet have a strong wedding network.
Value
 Question: “If couples who are already looking for wedding music could discover you, what would that be worth to you? Why?”
 Prediction: They will describe the value as visibility, being discovered, or gaining opportunities they would not otherwise find.
Persona
 Question: “How often are you looking for wedding gigs, and what do you usually do when you don't have one lined up?”
 Prediction: They will regularly want wedding opportunities but lack a consistent way to find them.
Capability
 Question: “If I showed you this app for a few seconds and then asked what it does, what would you say it's for?”
 Prediction: They should recognize that WedSinger connects wedding singers with couples looking for wedding music.
Capability
 Question: “If you wanted to find a wedding to sing at, what would you click first, and what would you expect to happen?”
 Prediction: They will look for a way to see potential couples and expect it to lead to wedding opportunities.
4. Design Justification and First Read
At first glance, the landing screen communicates WedSinger's purpose through “Get Found” and the supporting message about matching singers with couples searching for live wedding music. The “Show me couples” button makes the primary action clear. The wedding imagery and new logo also immediately signal the wedding-music context.
The landing screen prioritizes its main job rather than giving every element equal emphasis. Supporting information, such as free listing and no commission, is secondary. On the Matches screen, size, weight, and pills create visual hierarchy around the number of matches and the type of singer requested. Proximity groups information belonging to each wedding. On the Match Detail screen, the wedding details, couple's note, music request, and actions are grouped because they all help the singer evaluate the same match.
Screens 2 and 3 stay on mission by moving the singer from discovering opportunities → evaluating a match → taking action. The logo consistently returns to the landing screen, and the Match Detail screen has a clear back-to-Matches control.
What the AI Initially Got Wrong
The initial AI version was too wordy and lacked visual hierarchy. I shortened “Couples planning their reception can finally find you” to “Get Found” and changed “Show me couples looking now” to “Show me couples.” These changes make the primary value and action faster to understand.
The AI also presented “Seven couples looking for A singer and pianist” in one uniform style. I made the 7 larger and bolder and placed “Singer and Pianist” in a pill so the most important information can be understood at a glance. This improved signaling and visual hierarchy.
The AI named the app Duet, which was too specific to my persona and did not clearly communicate the product's purpose. I changed it to WedSinger, which immediately signals that the product connects wedding singers with wedding opportunities. I also added a logo featuring a wedding band with a music note, reinforcing both sides of the product.
Finally, the AI placed the logo in the top right on the Match Detail screen. I moved it to the top left for consistency and navigation, and added a back arrow with “Matches” in a pill so users can easily return to the Matches screen. These changes were driven by my design questions about signaling, hierarchy, grouping, and navigation.
Before and After
Before: The AI prototype used the name Duet, longer copy, and mostly uniform visual styling. Important information such as the number of matches and requested singer type did not stand out.

After: I changed the name to WedSinger, shortened the messaging, added a wedding/music logo, and used visual hierarchy, emphasis, and grouping to make key information easier to scan. I also improved navigation consistency between screens.
